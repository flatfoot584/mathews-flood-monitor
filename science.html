How to interpret these scores

Historical observed-weather benchmarks do not validate deployed forecasts. Stage 2 scores use the same historical record used to establish the depth relationship, not an independent holdout. A 1-meter LiDAR grid describes horizontal spacing, not vertical accuracy. Apparent threshold agreement does not prove sub-inch precision; elevation date, point type, vertical error and datum conversion need a documented survey audit.

Two-Stage Machine Learning Predictive Architecture

Decoupled estuarine hydrodynamic physics from hyper-local piecewise micro-topography.

Multi-Sensor Pipeline
1 Stage 1: Estuarine Hydrodynamic & Water Level Model

Predicts the regional water level on the Ware River gauge (WRVV2) by synthesizing astronomical tide tables, quadratic wind stress vectors (τw ∝ |U|U), barometric pressure tendencies (ΔP3h), rolling wind set-up memory (3h, 6h, 12h, 24h), and NOAA CBOFS hydrodynamic guidance.

Hydrodynamic Formulation:
Stagepred(t) = Tideast(t) + fGBM(τwx, τwy, ΔP3h, Memorywind)
2 Stage 2: Hyper-Local Piecewise Inundation Model

Translates the predicted or observed gauge stage into estimated flood depth in inches on property benchmarks, driveway access corridors, and neighborhood roads using our empirically discovered 3.99 ft tipping point and 10.95 in/ft linear inundation gradient.

Piecewise Inundation Law:
Depth(h) = max(0.0, 10.95 × Stageft − 43.69) inches
