Flood Risk Guide

Understanding the Flood Tiers & Tidal Physics

Clear, visual explanations of the 4 coastal flood severity tiers, vehicle driving limits, and plain-English translations of confusing marine terminology.

The 4 Risk Severity Tiers

Tier 0 Stage < 4.0' MLLW

Normal / Safe Conditions

Ground Flood Depth: 0.0 inches

Water is fully contained within natural marsh channels and roadside ditch beds. No road or lawn inundation.

Vehicles: All passenger cars, sedans, and delivery vans 100% passable.
Action: None required.
Tier 1 Stage 4.0' – 4.3' MLLW

Nuisance Flooding / Ditches Full

Ground Flood Depth: 1.0" – 4.0" (Low Spots)

Ditch beds are brim-full. Water begins backing up through driveway culvert pipes and creeps into low grassy depressions.

Vehicles: Main driveway passable; drive slowly through culvert dip.
Action: Keep pets inside; check ditch pipes for debris.
Tier 2 Stage 4.4' – 4.7' MLLW

Moderate Flooding / Driveway Impassable

Driveway Depth: 5.0" – 8.0"

Water sheets completely across the main driveway. Road edges disappear underwater.

Vehicles: Passenger cars & sedans BLOCKED. SUVs/trucks only.
Action: Move cars to high ground before high tide; plan travel around low tide.
Tier 3 Stage ≥ 4.8' MLLW

Severe Inundation / Property Submerged

Yard Depth: 9.0" – 15.0"+

"Island" conditions. Lawns, ditches, and access roads merge into continuous open water. Approaching garage apron.

Vehicles: All standard vehicles IMPASSABLE. High risk of stall or float.
Action: Emergency access only. Do not attempt to drive through floodwaters.

Vehicle Water Depth Danger Limits

Salt water is corrosive and electrically conductive. Even shallow depths cause catastrophic vehicle damage.

3 Inches
Tire Splash Zone

Passable for all passenger vehicles. Slow down to avoid throwing salt spray into engine compartments.

6 Inches
Sedan Danger Threshold

Reaches floorboards and exhaust pipes of passenger sedans. Stalls engines and ruins electronic modules.

12+ Inches
Buoyant / Floating Hazard

Water displaces vehicle weight. Cars float and can be swept off submerged road shoulders into ditch beds.

Demystifying Marine & Coastal Jargon

MLLW (Mean Lower Low Water) vs NAVD88

MLLW is a tidal datum referenced to the average lowest daily tide — boaters and NOAA tide tables use this because it keeps water levels as positive numbers. NAVD88 is a fixed national topographic elevation datum used by surveyors.

Local Mathews Conversion: NAVD88 (ft) = MLLW (ft) - 1.64 ft

Storm Surge Residual

The difference between the verified water level and the predicted astronomical tide. If NOAA predicted high tide at 2.5 ft, but the river reached 4.1 ft, the surge residual is +1.6 ft.

Along-Bay Wind Vector (Wind Set-Up)

The Chesapeake Bay runs approximately north-to-south (20° azimuth). Winds blowing from NNE, NE, and E act like a giant snowplow, forcing open-bay water down the Chesapeake and piling it into Mobjack Bay where it cannot escape.

Compound Pluvial Flooding

When high bay water plugs the drainage ditches, local rain has nowhere to drain. Even 2 inches of rain during a moderate high tide causes serious flooding that wouldn't occur on a dry day.
